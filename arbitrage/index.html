How an arb exists

Each price implies a probability: p = 1 / decimal_odds. A single book's prices always sum to more than 100%; the extra is its margin. If you take the best price for each outcome from different books and the sum drops below 100%, one set of stakes pays out more than it costs, whatever the result.

S = Σ 1/oᵢ → arb if S < 1 → ROI = 1/S − 1
stakeᵢ = T · (1/oᵢ) / S → every outcome returns T / S

The ARBX engine runs this on every market it sees: moneylines (2- and 3-way), each spread point and each total point. Exchange commission is taken off net winnings first: o' = 1 + (o − 1)(1 − c).

Middles

When books hang different lines, e.g. Over 44.5 at one book and Under 46.5 at another, both bets win if the final lands inside the window. ARBX estimates the chance of landing there from each sport's scoring spread and shows the expected value along with the worst case.

+EV (positive expected value)

A sharp book's prices, with the vig removed, are the closest thing to true probability. When a soft book pays more than that fair price, the bet has an edge. ARBX shows full, ½ and ¼ Kelly stakes: f* = (b·p − q) / b.

Stake optimizer

  • Equal payout: the same profit on every outcome.
  • Bias slider: moves profit onto one outcome while the others break even, so you ride the side you like and risk nothing.
  • Anchor a leg: type a stake into any leg (e.g. the book's max bet) and the other legs are sized to match it.
  • Rounding: searches every floor/ceil combination for the best guaranteed ROI with round-number stakes.

Funding your books

ARBX never holds, moves or bets money. You fund each sportsbook account directly (bank transfer, debit card, PayPal and so on) and place every bet yourself on the book's own site. Arbs close in seconds, so both books need money in them before the arb appears.

  • Record what's in each account on the Books tab. The scanner then works out how much of each arb you can actually fund, and the stake optimizer caps each leg at that book's balance.
  • Logging a bet takes the stakes out of those books. Settling it in the Ledger pays the winnings back into the book that won, or refunds every leg on a void.
  • Winnings pile up in some books and drain others. The rebalance plan tells you which to withdraw from and which to top up. Books can't transfer to each other, so each move is a withdrawal to your bank and then a deposit, and withdrawals can take days.

Workflow that actually banks

  • Place the harder leg first: the soft book with the stale line, or the one most likely to limit you. Then hedge right away.
  • Check the line on the book's site before you commit. Feeds lag by seconds, and the Graveyard shows how fast windows close.
  • Watch the rules: void and settlement rules differ (overtime, retirements, 3-way vs 2-way). A mismatch can turn an arb into exposure.
  • Log every bet in the Ledger and settle it, so your realized ROI is measured instead of guessed.
Reality check. Arbitrage is mathematically risk-free but not operationally risk-free. Books cancel "palpable error" bets, cap stakes, and limit accounts that only take stale lines.
